# Words

Most "AI-made" pages give themselves away through words before anything else. A model reaches for
the phrase that fits every business; good copy is the fact that fits only this one. So the
specifics must come from the user, and every banned habit needs a plain replacement, or a near
synonym sneaks back in.

## The basics

- Say what it is, for whom, and what happens next, in plain words a customer would use.
- Facts over adjectives: replace "fast" with the time, "affordable" with the price, "trusted" with
  who uses it, only when the user gave those facts.
- Short is good, but not a drumbeat of fragments. Let one sentence per section run normally.
- Buttons say the action: "Book a call", "Add spend", "Log today's food".
- Microcopy is copy too: empty states, errors and confirmations are written with the same care.

## Banned words (each one is a tell)

delve, tapestry, testament to, realm, embark, beacon, multifaceted, paradigm, myriad, plethora,
meticulous, intricate, garner, boasts (for "has"), elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, harness,
supercharge, seamless, effortless, cutting-edge, revolutionary, transformative, holistic,
next-level, world-class, best-in-class, game-changer, state-of-the-art, bespoke, synergy, leverage,
reimagine, powerful, robust, nestled, unparalleled.

A word that is literally true for the trade is fine (a bespoke tailor, a climbing harness), and so
is the client's own brand name.

## Stock phrases (never)

"In today's fast-paced world", "Unlock the power of", "The future of X is here", "Say goodbye to",
"Whether you're A or B", "Join thousands of", "Trusted by teams worldwide", "Imagine a world
where", "Ready to transform your X?", "Look no further", "one-stop shop", "X made simple", "at your
fingertips", "in just a few clicks", "It's not just X, it's Y", "Here's the thing", "Welcome back,
[name]! 👋".

## Shapes that give it away

- A trailing "-ing" clause on a fact: "We send the quote, ensuring a smooth start." Cut it.
- The question answered at once: "The result? Three hours saved." Write the sentence plainly.
- The colon reveal: "One thing changed: everything." Say what changed.
- "Not X, but Y" contrast frames, and their substitutes "rather than", "instead of", "without the".
  Use at most two on a page. State the positive fact.
- Runs of negations: "No forms. No waiting. No hassle." One at most, only if each is checkable.
- Two-fragment headings on every section: "Twelve rooms. Three ways to stay." Once at most.
- Em dashes everywhere. Use full stops, commas, colons or brackets.
- Exclamation marks in body copy. Emoji as bullets.
- Dead metaphors: landscape, ecosystem, journey (unless travel), hub, "where X meets Y", "in the
  heart of".

## The subtle tells

- **Invented specifics.** "Batch No. 047", "38% fewer errors", "6:12 a.m. from Room 9": detail made
  up because detail persuades. Use only the user's facts; otherwise write a plainer line.
- **The stat strip.** A row of four big round figures under the hero. Put each real figure into
  the sentence it proves.
- **Polished testimonials.** Three tidy quotes with first names and a punchline. Only quotes the
  user supplied, word for word.
- **The default cast.** Priya, Marcus, Sarah J., Acme, Northwind, Lumen, Apex. For sample data,
  pick names that fit the place and trade (a UK catering parts firm's customers: real-sounding
  local cafes and hotels).
- **Virtue words about yourself:** honest, genuinely, truly, simply, thoughtful, carefully.
  Show it with a fact instead.
- **The dry closer.** A quip or moral at the end of every paragraph. End on information.
- **Template sections.** Small label + big claim + lede + three cards, every section. Shape each
  section by what it actually needs to say.
- **The page talking about itself.** "Below you'll find", "This section shows". Never.
- **A stale year** in the footer. Use the current year.

## Not tells (leave alone)

Plain "is" and "has". Perfect grammar. A list of three when there are three things. Selling on a
sales page. Copy the user wrote themselves.
